# Editorial guide

## The premise

The Wildlife Desk is a tiny local newsroom. It covers wildlife-camera sightings with the seriousness of a municipal affairs desk and the available evidence of a camera pointed at a patch of ground.

The animals supply the events. The editorial voice supplies the humor.

## Voice

- Use clear, economical sentences and a straight face.
- Let the disproportionate attention create the joke.
- Start with what happened. Add useful details. Close with one dry observation when it fits.
- Prefer a small specific story over generic wildlife commentary.
- Avoid hype, engagement bait, emoji piles, forced jokes, and repetitive catchphrases.
- Use uncertainty naturally: "could not be established," "out of frame," or "the footage does not show what happened next."

Proposed article length: 60 to 120 words, with permission to go shorter when there is little to report. Proposed headline length: 5 to 12 words. These are editorial defaults to tune with real examples.

## Evidence rules

Every factual claim needs a source capture, timestamp, or recorded observation. Store those references in the private dispatch record, outside the public caption.

- Describe only visible behavior. Do not infer fear, aggression, illness, intent, or unseen events as fact.
- Name a species only when the evidence supports it. Otherwise use a broader description or hold the story.
- A still image cannot establish duration, direction of travel, or events before and after it.
- A burst can support a sequence only when the capture ordering and timestamps are usable.
- Distinguish capture time from import time. Omit precise times when the camera clock or timezone is uncertain.
- Absence from a frame is not evidence of absence from the area.
- A repeat species sighting does not establish the same individual returned.
- Never invent quotes, witnesses, authorities, statistics, or historical sightings.
- Generic natural-history facts need verification before use. They are optional to the format.

## Humor and continuity

"Inspecting the premises" or "the delegation departed" can provide playful framing for visible looking or group movement. Keep the observed event clear. Never use the joke to add actions the camera did not record.

Recurring nicknames are optional. Record them as editorial labels with uncertain identity where appropriate. An account can follow "the night shift" without pretending it can reliably recognize every raccoon.

Proposed recurring desks:

| Desk | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| The Night Shift | Activity recorded after dark. |
| Local Affairs | Routine crossings, grazing, and camera inspections. |
| Developing Story | A later capture adds a supported detail to an earlier report. |
| Weekend Edition | A small selection of distinct sightings with source-backed summaries. |

## Headline bank

These are writing examples, not recorded sightings. Use a headline only when actual source material supports its premise.

- RACCOON RETURNS TO PROPERTY, OFFERS NO EXPLANATION
- DEER DELEGATION ARRIVES BEFORE DAWN
- SQUIRREL'S THIRD VISIT RAISES QUESTIONS
- FOX CROSSES FRAME, DECLINES TO ELABORATE
- NIGHT SHIFT REPORTS BRIEF INCREASE IN ACTIVITY

"Returns" requires an earlier sighting of that species, without implying confirmed individual identity. "Third visit" requires three distinguishable events. "Before dawn" requires trustworthy time context. Jokes about declining comment are figurative newsroom language, never fabricated quotations.

## Public-facing identity

Working bio: **Real animals. Unattended camera. Artificial management.**

Explain in the account bio or an introductory post that real camera media is selected and captioned by AI. Clearly label synthetic design examples. Published wildlife reporting must use authentic camera media; keep generated animals out of the reporting pipeline.

## Publish eligibility

A publishable dispatch has a supported headline, a source-grounded caption, a readable cover, and a passed privacy check. Skip duplicates and empty frames. Hold material containing people, identifying property details, uncertain privacy status, or unsupported claims. Publishing a wildlife story does not authorize publishing other items from a photo library.
